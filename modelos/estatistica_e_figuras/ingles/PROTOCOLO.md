# Protocol — bilingual statistical demonstration

## Purpose
Demonstrate a minimal technical pattern for statistics, visualisation, tables, and R exports. The example does not represent patients, published studies, clinical estimates, or inference about a real population.

## Data sources and unit
All data are generated by the script with a fixed seed. The descriptive-table unit is a simulated observation; the forest-plot unit is an artificial study. No external or human data are imported.

## Outcomes and parameters
The script generates an arbitrary continuous measure and an artificial binary indicator for Table 1; six artificial estimates and standard errors for an REML model with Hartung–Knapp adjustment; and a simple logistic model on generated data to show calibration on a synthetic train/test split.

## Procedures
Table 1 reports the median and interquartile range for the measure and n (%) for the indicator. No hypothesis tests or p-values are calculated. The measure includes artificial missingness, displayed as its own category. The forest plot uses normal 95% intervals for individual effects and the interval returned by the random-effects model with Hartung–Knapp adjustment. The calibration curve groups predictions into deciles and shows exact binomial intervals by group.

## Reproducibility and languages
One fixed seed drives the data, and a second fixed value derives the prediction example. Code and technical CSV data stay shared; manuscripts, tables, figures, captions, and alternative text have native Portuguese and English versions, with the same Portuguese filename.

## Limits
Sample size, proportions, and effects are arbitrary teaching choices. Binned calibration can be unstable and loses information; synthetic validation does not assess transportability, clinical performance, or decision utility. Six studies do not support reliable detection of publication bias, meta-regression, or conclusions about heterogeneity. This model does not replace a registered protocol, analysis plan, methodological expertise, clinical review, or Evidence Lock.

# Research protocol questionnaire

Use this guide to turn an open research question into a protocol that can be frozen and entered into a registry. Do not fill gaps by inferring from older work. For each item, record the answer, source/decision, owner, and state: **answered**, **not applicable (explain)**, or **pending**. Pending decisions that affect the question, eligibility, outcomes, sample, collection, analysis, ethics, or search block protocol freeze.

## A. Status and scope

- Project/internal ID and protocol version:
- Date and owner of this version:
- Current stage: preparation/bootstrap; protocol drafting; protocol frozen; registration submitted; final registration confirmed; search/data collection started; screening; extraction; analysis; discovery; writing. Mark documented facts only.
- What has already happened, when, by whom, and with what evidence? Do not relabel past work as prospective.
- Is there an existing protocol, OSF project, manuscript, pending submission, or previous registration? Record URL/ID/status; none alone is an eligible final registration.
- Work type: evidence review; primary study; trial; observational study; prediction/ML study; secondary study; simulation; preclinical; other (specify).
- Open decision questions:

## B. Identification, authorship, and support

- Short and full title:
- Is this a new registration, update, replication, or amendment? Existing ID/history:
- Institutions, responsible contact, all planned authors, and contributions:
- Who is accountable for protocol integrity and who approves changes?
- Collaborators, statistical methods, information specialist/librarian, stakeholders/patients:
- Funding, material support, sponsor, and each party's role in the question, design, conduct, analysis, and publication:
- Financial, professional, intellectual, or other conflicts, including none declared:
- Protocol/work languages; plan to translate while preserving the same IDs, outcomes, and numbers:

## C. Review type and question

- Which synthesis answers the question? Effects systematic review; scoping; narrative; rapid; umbrella/overview; living; diagnostic accuracy; prognosis; network meta-analysis (NMA); qualitative review; other.
- Why does this type fit the question, evidence base, and decision? Which alternative type was considered and rejected, and why?
- Is the question about intervention/effect, diagnosis, prevalence, exposure/etiology, prognosis, experience, implementation, mapping, or network comparisons?
- Use the applicable framework and complete only relevant elements:
  - **PICO/PICOS:** population, intervention/exposure if relevant, comparator, outcomes, and eligible design.
  - **PCC:** population, concept, and context (often used for scoping reviews).
  - **PECO:** population, exposure, comparator, and outcome (etiologic question).
- Full question, primary objective, and secondary objectives:
- Eligible unit (person, group, study, review, model, test, registry):
- Intended causal, descriptive, diagnostic, or predictive interpretation; what the question cannot establish:

## D. Rationale and outcomes

- What is known and what specific gap motivates the work? Which previous reviews/protocols were found, when, and how does this work differ?
- Primary outcome with operational definition, instrument/code, unit, favorable direction, time window, and rule for multiple measures:
- Secondary and exploratory outcomes, kept separate from the primary:
- Important clinical difference or decision threshold and its rationale/source, if applicable:
- For diagnostic accuracy: target condition, index test, threshold(s), reference standard, timing, and use setting:
- For prognosis: event, horizon, time zero, source population, competing events, and intended action:
- For NMA: nodes, grouped doses/versions, comparisons, outcomes, and effect modifiers supporting transitivity:
- For scoping/narrative work: mapping categories or themes, without promising effect inference:

## E. Eligibility

- Population, condition, age/range, context, and setting included/excluded:
- Intervention, exposure, index test, comparator/reference, as applicable:
- Outcomes and designs included; design rationale:
- Report/source types: article, preprint, thesis, registry, report, abstract, individual data; allowed publication/status:
- Time, language, geography, and other restrictions. For each limit, rationale and risk of missing evidence:
- Study-level versus report-level exclusions; handling multiple reports of one study:
- Rules for mixed populations, overlapping data, subgroups, crossover, cluster, multiple arms, and duplicates:
- Ambiguous eligibility cases, author contact, deadline, and decision owner:

## F. Sources, search, and selection

- Full databases and platforms (not names alone), study registries, grey literature, websites, contacts, reference lists, and forward citation search:
- Reproducible strategy for each source; attach a complete search for at least one database, including fields, operators, and limits:
- Coverage dates and planned search date; who runs and reviews the strategy:
- Deduplication, software/version, identifiers, and audit trail:
- Title/abstract and full-text screening: independent reviewers, pilot, conflict resolution, third reviewer, and authorized automation:
- If any step uses a single reviewer or shortcut, say which, why, how it will be checked, and how the limitation will be reported:
- Standard full-text exclusion reasons and PRISMA reconciliation:
- Search update before publication; update criteria and end date:

## G. Extraction and data management

- Extraction unit and data form/code; pilot, dictionary, version, and change control:
- Who extracts, number of reviewers, independence, checking, and disagreement resolution:
- Design, participants, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcomes, denominators, effects, uncertainty, funding, conflicts, protocol/registry, and risk-of-bias variables:
- Rules for adjusted versus unadjusted estimates, time points, measures, correlations, missing summary statistics, and conversions:
- Individual data, author contact, sharing, access, security, retention, de-identification, and legal/ethics basis:
- Identification of multiple reports for the same study and prevention of double counting:
- For a primary study: instrument/CRF, collection, validation, dictionary, audit, and protection of original data:

## H. Risk of bias, certainty, and validity

- Tool appropriate to design/question (e.g., RoB 2, ROBINS-I, QUADAS-3 v1.2; QUADAS-2 only for an already specified historical assessment or dated requirement; QUIPS, PROBAST/PROBAST+AI, JBI tool); version, domains, and rationale:
- Assessors, number, independence, pilot, consensus, and adjudication:
- Outcome/result-level judgment where required; do not treat scores as automatic truth or exclude without an a priori rule:
- Certainty of evidence (e.g., GRADE/CINeMA) or reason not to assess; domains, unit, and team:
- Reporting/publication bias, missing data, protocol/registry, and unpublished results:
- Internal, external, and measurement validity, applicability, fragile assumptions, and limits:

## I. Synthesis and statistical plan

- Narrative, tabular, qualitative, quantitative, or combined synthesis; clinical/methodological compatibility rule:
- Effect measure and scale (RR, OR, HR, mean difference, SMD, sensitivity/specificity, or other), direction, estimand, and target population:
- Model, estimator, interval, inferential method, and planned software/packages/versions; explain each choice:
- For meta-analysis: heterogeneity, tau-squared, prediction interval when useful, weights, dependent studies, and multi-arm effects:
- Meta-regression/subgroups: hypotheses and few prespecified moderators; small-study-count limits; exploratory analyses kept separate:
- Missing data: plausible mechanism, primary analysis, methods, assumptions, and sensitivity; missingness is not one universal case:
- Multiplicity: hypothesis family, outcome hierarchy, control, or exploratory interpretation:
- Sensitivity, robustness, influence, assumptions, diagnostics, and criteria not to pool:
- Publication bias/small-study effects: method, minimum study-count precondition, and limited interpretation; do not use a test to “prove no” bias:
- Data that cannot be pooled, discordant results, and narrative decisions:

## J. Primary-study and sample branch

Complete this section if the protocol includes primary data collection/study; for a review, write “not applicable” with a reason.

- Design (randomized/cluster/crossover trial, cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, diagnostic, prognostic, prediction, qualitative, simulation, etc.) and allocation/analysis unit:
- Primary estimand, population, condition/intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, and horizon:
- Source/target population, recruitment/sampling, criteria, location, schedule, and flow:
- Sample size: goal (precision, power, events, validation, saturation), parameters/sources, formula/code, losses, and scenarios:
- Randomization, concealment, blinding, adherence, contamination, co-interventions, clustering, and intraclass correlation:
- Analysis matched to estimand; adjustment, confounders, covariate selection, interactions, weights, censoring, and competing risks:
- Safety/adverse events, monitoring, stopping rule, and owner, where relevant:

## K. Prediction/ML branch

Complete this section when developing, validating, updating, or comparing a prediction model; do not mix prediction with causal inference.

- Intended use, user, decision, population, prediction time, horizon, outcome, and threshold:
- Predictors available at the intended use time, sources, preprocessing, and variable handling:
- Data separation at the correct level (person/site/time), external validation, and update plan:
- Leakage prevention: split train/validation/test before imputation, feature selection, normalization, feature engineering, resampling, tuning, and selection; learn transformations on training data only within each fold:
- Repeated people, sites, time series, families, or studies; partition to prevent group leakage:
- Simple comparator, reference model, selection/tuning, nested resampling, and prespecified metrics:
- Effective sample size, events per parameter/complexity, prevalence, optimism, and uncertainty:
- Discrimination, calibration (curve/intercept/slope), error, clinical utility, and planned subgroups/fairness:
- Missing data, shift, drift, explainability, and deployment limits:
- Applicable PROBAST+AI and TRIPOD+AI; reporting completeness is not low risk of bias or clinical benefit:

## L. Ethics, transparency, authorship, and amendments

- Ethics/IRB approval, waiver, or written institutional determination, number/date; consent/assent:
- For a randomized trial, current guideline/version: SPIRIT 2025 for the protocol and CONSORT 2025 for reporting, with the relevant extension; older versions only for historical context or a dated requirement:
- Trial registries and other legal, regulatory, or institutional obligations beyond PROSPERO/OSF:
- Privacy, sensitive data, security, sharing, licence, and retention:
- Conflicts, funding, sponsor role, and analytical independence:
- Planned contributions/authorship, authorship criteria, data/code owners, and dispute process:
- Plan to share protocol, materials, code, data, and negative results; justified restrictions:
- Amendment record: date, version/fingerprint, change, reason, impact, owner, ethics/registry approval, stage already reached, and disclosure:
- Never rewrite a frozen protocol to make a later decision appear prospective. Do not erase deviations; report them.

## M. Registration, freeze, and unresolved questions

- Justified choice: PROSPERO (only if currently eligible), OSF Registries, or additional mandatory registry:
- Final protocol checked against registration form and attachments, version, and SHA-256:
- Date before any corpus, screening, extraction, analysis, discovery, or scientific writing:
- Can the reviewer attest, after checking ledgers, manifests, legacy files, and work outside the pipeline, that no scientific activity began before final registration? Attach the reconciliation; do not infer this from a missing automated marker:
- Registration submitted, pending, approved/final, public, or embargoed? Distinguish these states; local policy requires confirmed final status:
- Final registry identifier and canonical URL; do not enter a fictitious value:
- Dated local snapshot of the final record, SHA-256, named reviewer, review date, and protocol-match confirmation:
- Unresolved questions, owner, deadline, and blocking condition they resolve:
- Confirm no scientific decision was inferred from an old question without current evidence:
